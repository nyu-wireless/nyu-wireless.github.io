**Theodore (Ted) S. Rappaport**, the David Lee/Ernst Weber Professor of
Electrical and Computer Engineering at the NYU Tandon School of Engineering
and founding director of NYU WIRELESS, has been elected to the American
Academy of Arts and Sciences, one of the country's oldest and most
distinguished honorary societies.

He is recognized for a body of work that has shaped nearly every generation of
modern wireless communications: from the early technical foundations behind
Wi-Fi and digital cellular service, to the millimeter-wave research that
underpins 5G, to the spectrum studies now guiding 6G.

The Academy was founded in 1780 by John Adams, John Hancock and other
scholar-patriots. It elects leaders from the academic, business and government
sectors who address the major challenges facing society. Past members include
Benjamin Franklin, Albert Einstein, Margaret Mead and Martin Luther King, Jr.
The 2026 class, which also includes Nicole R. Fleetwood of NYU Steinhardt and
Vlad Vicol of Courant, is being inducted in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in
October.

## A career that built the wireless era

Rappaport is perhaps best known for showing, more than a decade ago, that
millimeter-wave frequencies, once dismissed as unusable for mobile
communications, could carry the high-capacity signals that 5G now depends on.
His 2013 paper, *"Millimeter Wave Mobile Communications for 5G Cellular: It
Will Work!"*, has been called a founding document of the technology.

His contributions go well beyond that breakthrough:

- **Early foundations.** With his students, he helped lay the technical
  groundwork for Wi-Fi, the first digital cellular standards used in the
  United States, and the E-911 location systems that let emergency responders
  find callers from any mobile phone.
- **Network design.** He developed widely used methods for designing wireless
  networks inside buildings, and a probability distribution that models modern
  wireless channels.
- **Energy efficiency.** Most recently, he developed Waste Factor, a unifying
  mathematical framework for evaluating energy efficiency and sustainability
  in any communications link.

Rappaport founded NYU WIRELESS in 2012 as one of the first U.S. academic
centers to bring together engineering, computer science and medicine. He holds
appointments in NYU Tandon's Department of Electrical and Computer
Engineering, Courant's Department of Computer Science, and the Department of
Radiology at the NYU Grossman School of Medicine. He is the author or
co-author of more than 400 papers and 20 books, including the most widely
cited textbooks on wireless communications, smart antennas, communication
systems simulation and millimeter-wave communications. His books have been
translated into eight languages.

## Recent honors

The election follows several other recent honors. In January 2026, Rappaport
was named a Life Fellow of the IEEE and a Fellow of the Association for
Computing Machinery, placing him among the few scholars honored at the highest
level by the leading societies in both electrical engineering and computer
science. He is also a member of the U.S. National Academy of Engineering and
a Fellow of the National Academy of Inventors, and he was the first career
academic inducted into the Wireless Hall of Fame.

## Joining NYU Tandon colleagues in the Academy

Rappaport joins a group of current and former NYU Tandon faculty who have been
named Members of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences over the past
half-century:

- **Katepalli R. Sreenivasan**, NYU Tandon Dean Emeritus and Eugene Kleiner
  Professor of Innovation in Mechanical Engineering, a leading scholar of
  turbulence and complex fluid flows.
- **Juan de Pablo**, Anne and Joel Ehrenkranz Executive Vice President for
  Global Science and Technology at NYU and Executive Dean of NYU Tandon, a
  chemical engineer whose research spans soft materials, polymers and
  computational molecular science.
- **Dora E. Angelaki**, Director of NYU's Center for Neural Science, whose
  work lies at the intersection of perception, cognition and engineering.
- **David Pine**, Silver Professor of Physics and Mathematics, a pioneer in
  the design of colloidal materials and self-assembling structures.
- **Jeffrey A. Hubbell**, VP of Bioengineering Strategy and head of the
  Initiative to Advance Engineering and Health, a leader in immunoengineering
  and regenerative medicine.

> &mdash; Juan de Pablo, Member of the Academy since 2011
